# Central Care

Central Care is the operating platform for a central vacuum business. It brings homeowners, field technicians, and the office onto one shared service: request a visit, agree a price, book the appointment, complete the work, and buy equipment or parts from the same company.

The platform exists so a homeowner does not have to phone the office, wait on a paper quote, and then chase a technician. The office keeps pricing, scheduling, and payment under its control. The technician arrives with a clear job, records what was found and what was done, and leaves a record the customer can confirm.

Currency is Canadian dollars. Service addresses default to Canada. The business publishes its name, phone, support email, address, service area, logo, and public landing image so the storefront and apps present one company.

---

## Who it is for

**Homeowners** use the customer experience to book service, follow a visit, and shop. They create an account, save home addresses, and choose how they want to be notified.

**Technicians** use the field experience on a mobile app. They see only the jobs the office has assigned to them. They travel to the home, document the system, and submit a service report. They do not set prices, choose their own jobs, or take payment.

**The office** uses the admin dashboard to run the business. Staff review new requests, send quotes, decide price discussions, assign technicians, watch the calendar, fulfill shop orders, and close payment only after the work is confirmed.

A public visitor who is not signed in can still reach the company through the contact form and see the published business identity on the storefront.

---

## What the platform delivers

Central Care delivers four outcomes.

1. **A managed service visit.** The homeowner describes the problem, the office prices it, both sides agree, a technician is booked, the work is documented, and the customer confirms it before the card is charged.
2. **A parts and equipment shop.** The same customer can browse the catalog, keep a cart, pay securely, track the order, and request a return.
3. **A field record of every system.** Each visit can capture the power unit, where it sits, its condition, inlet counts by floor, and photos taken before and after the work.
4. **One conversation around the job.** Customer and technician can message, place a video call, and the customer can follow the technician’s live location while the visit is scheduled or underway.

## The service experience

Service covers two kinds of work.

**Maintenance** includes a unit that will not turn on or shut off, a clogged system, low suction, a retractable hose that will not pull out or retract, a broken inlet valve or vacuum port, and a general service or parts request.

**Installation** includes a new system, a custom fit, a system upgrade, and architectural work.

### How a visit moves

The homeowner chooses a service type and the issue that best matches the problem, picks a saved address, describes what is happening, and may suggest a preferred day and time window. Photos and short videos of the unit, inlet, or the noise can be attached so the office can judge the job before anyone travels.

The office reviews the request. When it is ready, it sends one written quote. The quote shows labor, parts, tax, any discount, and a total, and it states how long the offer remains valid. Opening the quote tells the office the customer has seen it.

The customer then has three choices.

- **Accept** the quote and agree to the current terms.
- **Propose a different total**, with an optional note. The office approves or declines that proposal. The original list price stays on record. If the office agrees, the agreed amount becomes the amount that will be held on the card.
- **Decline** the quote. The request returns to the office for review.

Accepting the quote does not charge the card. It places a hold for the agreed amount through a secure payment page. The business never asks the customer to type a card number into Central Care itself. The office can book the visit only after that hold is in place.

The customer does not pick the technician or lock the appointment time. The office assigns a verified, available technician and sets the start and end of the visit. Both the customer and the technician are notified. From that point the customer can see who is coming and when.

On the day, the technician marks the visit as started, may share live location, and can call or message the customer. During the visit the technician records the equipment, inlet counts, notes, and before-and-after photos, then submits a service report: what was repaired, the work performed, parts used, arrival and departure, and whether a follow-up is needed.

The customer reads that report and confirms the work. Only then does the office complete the charge against the earlier hold. The visit is finished.

The customer may cancel while the request is still waiting, under review, quoted, accepted, or scheduled. Once the technician has started the work, cancellation is no longer available. The office can also cancel a request before work begins. Every change of status is kept so the history of the visit is clear.

## The shop experience

The shop sells central vacuum equipment and related products. Each product can show a name, description, category, price, photos, features, specifications, warranty, and shipping information. Customers can search, filter by category and price, and limit the list to items that are in stock.

A signed-in customer can add items to a persistent cart or buy a product immediately. Totals can be reviewed before payment. Tax applies only to taxable products. Shipping is not billed as a separate fee. Checkout uses a saved address; guests cannot pay.

Payment opens on a hosted, secure checkout page. An order is paid only when the payment provider confirms it. Returning to the site after checkout is not itself proof of payment. Stock is held when checkout starts and released if payment does not complete.

After payment, the office moves the order through processing, shipment, and delivery, and can add a carrier and tracking number. The customer sees active and completed orders, can cancel an order that has not been paid, request a return with a reason, and buy the same items again. The office reviews return requests, can attach a return label, and records whether the return is approved, received, or refunded.

## People, trust, and communication

**Accounts.** Customers and technicians register with email and password. A verification code is sent by email before the account is active. Forgotten passwords are reset the same way. Each person can keep a profile photo, phone number, and notification preferences for email and in-app alerts. First-time use includes a short onboarding step.

**Technician approval.** A new technician states a service area, skills, and optional license and experience. The office reviews that registration and approves or declines it before the person can be assigned to homes. Technicians can mark themselves available or unavailable. Completed work contributes to a rating the office and the technician can see.

**Messages and calls.** Each service visit has its own conversation between the customer and the assigned technician. Either side can send messages and attachments. Either side, or the office, can start a video call tied to that visit. Calls are time-limited and can be refreshed or ended.

**Live location.** While a job is scheduled or in progress, the assigned technician can share location so the customer can follow the visit. Location is tied to that job, not to general tracking of the technician’s day.

**Notifications.** People receive in-app alerts for events that affect them, such as a new quote, a scheduled visit, or a new message. The office can also send a broadcast to selected groups. Email is used for account verification, password reset, and public contact messages.

**Public contact.** Anyone can send the office a message with a name, email, optional phone, the service they are asking about, and the message itself. The office receives it by email and as an in-app alert.

**Business identity.** The office maintains the public face of the company: business name, office phone, support email, address, service area, logo, and the image used on the landing page.

## What the office sees

The dashboard is the daily operating picture.

- New requests waiting for review
- Quotes waiting for a customer response
- Visits scheduled today and visits currently in progress
- Reports waiting for the office to close payment
- Service revenue for the month
- Shop orders waiting to ship
- Payments that failed
- How many technicians are active, available, and already booked today

Staff can open a calendar by day, week, or month, review recent requests and recent orders, and see which service issues come up most often. They can export business and service reports.

The office also keeps the customer’s equipment history: unit number, manufacturer, model, serial number, location, condition, extra features, photos, and how many inlets of each type sit on each floor. That record belongs to the customer and can be attached to a visit, so the next technician is not starting from a blank page.

## How the three roles share one visit

| Moment | Homeowner | Office | Technician |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Request | Describes the problem and attaches photos or video | Reviews the request | Does not see it yet |
| Price | Accepts, proposes another total, or declines | Sends the quote and decides any proposal | Does not price the job |
| Payment hold | Authorizes the card for the agreed amount | Books only after the hold is in place | Does not take payment |
| Appointment | Sees who is coming and when | Assigns a verified technician and the time window | Sees the job on Today or Upcoming |
| Visit | Messages, calls, and follows live location | Watches status | Starts the job, records the system, submits the report |
| Close | Confirms the completed work | Charges the hold after that confirmation | May still update the report until the visit is closed |

The shop follows a separate path. The office fulfills orders and handles returns. Technicians are not part of checkout.

## What a finished visit leaves behind

A completed request is more than a paid invoice. It leaves:

- The original description, preferred timing, and the photos or video the homeowner sent
- The quote, any price discussion, the terms the customer accepted, and the amount that was held and later charged
- The assigned technician and the scheduled window
- The conversation, call history, and location updates from the visit
- Equipment details, inlet counts, and before-and-after photos
- The service report and the customer’s confirmation
- A status history of who moved the visit forward and when

A completed shop order leaves the items, prices, tax, shipping address, payment result, shipment tracking, and any return.

---

## Boundaries the platform keeps

These boundaries are part of the service, not limits to work around.

- The office owns pricing, technician assignment, and the appointment window.
- The technician owns the visit record: notes, photos, equipment, and the service report.
- The customer owns the decision to accept a price, authorize a hold, confirm the work, and cancel before work starts.
- A service charge is completed only after the customer confirms the report.
- A shop order is paid only when the payment provider confirms it.
- Card details stay on the payment provider’s page.
- A technician sees assigned jobs only, and only after the office has verified the technician.
- Live location is shared for an active assigned visit, not as ongoing personal tracking.

## The promise of the platform

Central Care is the place a household books and follows central vacuum care, the place a technician runs a clear job, and the place the office prices, schedules, fulfills, and closes the work with a record both sides can trust.
